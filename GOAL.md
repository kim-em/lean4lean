## The brief: think differently

Two careful attempts and a 14k-line study have not closed it. That is not evidence that their routes are dead ends: what they established is checked and reusable, and the remaining obligation is sharp enough that a harder push along the confluence route (a standardisation or developments argument for the join, a stronger descent invariant) or the strong-derivation route may well finish it. Treat those as live options alongside the new angles below, not as excluded. Spend your first effort generating a range of angles, old and new, and testing each for a fatal flaw before committing to it; keep a written list of ideas with a one-line verdict each. Be bold and do not pre-judge by apparent size: an idea is rejected only by a concrete obstruction you have checked, never because it looks like too much work, needs a new theory, or "would take months" (estimates of that kind have been wrong in both directions on this project, and the budget is the owner's to spend, not yours to protect). Some directions nobody has tried here, to add to the two existing routes (reject any of them with a reason, add your own):

- **Finite developments instead of normalisation.** The residue is a matched beta-redex pair in a join. Complete developments of a marked redex set terminate in any lambda calculus, normalising or not (the finite developments theorem). Can the confluence join of two lifted terms be chosen as complete developments of the redexes created by the descent, so that the matched pair is contracted, with created redexes handled by a second marking? Standardisation (leftmost-outermost, Curry–Feys/Takahashi) is the same family of tools and gives canonical joins without normalisation.
- **Change what the checker proof needs, not the theorem.** Strengthening is used only to move cache facts and equivalence-manager facts out of a binder's scope (`VContext.strengthening` in `Verify/TypeChecker/Basic.lean`, `lowerClosedBody`, `TrExprS.lowerClosedBV`, `State.WF.eqv_uniq`, two sites in `Verify/Environment/Primitive/Recursion.lean`). The checker's own derivation of a cached fact is algorithmic and syntactic: a comparison of two terms that do not mention the binder never inspects the binder (it looks up only the free variables that occur). So an *algorithmic* strengthening theorem, by induction on the executable's derivation (the `M`/`RecM` monad runs, already modelled in `Verify/`), might be provable where the declarative one is not, and the cache invariant could store the algorithmic fact or a derivation shape rather than a declarative `IsDefEqU`. This changes `Verify/` proofs, not the executable. Check whether the equivalence manager's union-find facts admit the same treatment.
- **Strong derivations with a measure.** `IsDefEqStrong` (`Theory/Typing/Strong.lean`) annotates every step with typings. Is there a cut-elimination-style argument on strong derivations of lifted equations (height, or the proof-erased size `μ` of route B's `ProofErasedSize.lean`) that avoids normalisation because every conversion step inside types is itself a smaller strong derivation of lifted terms? Route A's log §5 explains where the obvious measures stall; look for a different induction order (e.g. on the type's derivation first).
- **Exploit canonical `Eq` for real.** Neither attempt used it beyond confluence. With `Eq`, a definitional equality between propositions typed below gives a *propositional* equality term internal to the theory; can the typed front be reduced to transporting along such terms, or to an internal statement whose proof the theory itself supplies (the extraction `P v` from a proof of `Eq`, which is what kills the countermodel)? What exactly does the countermodel need that canonical `Eq` destroys, and can that be turned into an invariant of derivations?
- **Semantic interpretations other than the observation model.** A Kripke/realisability model indexed by contexts in which the interpretation of a term depends only on its free variables by construction (the study's `Kripke.lean` sketches one); a reducibility-candidates argument restricted to the front; or a set-theoretic model in the style of Carneiro's thesis "The Type Theory of Lean" (check what the thesis proves about unused variables and strengthening, and why its argument does or does not transfer to a calculus with proof irrelevance and large eliminating singletons). Note the warning from route B: any model in which equality is "equal under all valuations" makes strengthening false when the binder's type is empty, so a working model must be syntactic in some respect.
- **Restrict, then generalise.** Prove `PropFront` for a fragment (first-order propositions; propositions whose lifts are beta-normal; terms whose join needs no created redex) and see whether the general case reduces to the fragment by a translation the theory itself provides.
- **Look for a counterexample seriously.** Two propositions typed and inhabited below whose lifts are convertible above but not below, in an environment with canonical `Eq`. Use the countermodel's mechanism (singleton eliminations, proof irrelevance) and ask what canonical `Eq` leaves of it. A counterexample would end the question as decisively as a proof.
